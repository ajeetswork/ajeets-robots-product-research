# Accessibility Information Review Framework

> For Ajeet's Robots Printify product listings
>
> **Scope:** This framework audits whether a product listing *communicates clearly* — whether a customer can understand what they're buying, how it fits, how to care for it, what it works with, and what to expect physically when handling it.
>
> **This is NOT a medical accessibility audit.** We do not evaluate products against ADA, WCAG, Section 508, EN 301 549, or any clinical/disability standard. We do not certify products as "accessible," "ADA compliant," "suitable for persons with disabilities," or make any health/medical claims. We audit *information clarity* only.
>
> **No medical claims. No assistive-technology claims. No accessibility certification claims.** If a product happens to have features that some customers may find helpful (e.g., a tear-away label, a large handle, lightweight construction), describe the *physical attribute factually* — do not connect it to a medical condition, disability, or accessibility outcome.

---

## 1. When to use this framework

- Before publishing a new Printify product (`visible: false → true`)
- Before promoting / featuring an existing product (ads, blog posts, email campaigns, group orders)
- During periodic catalog reviews
- When customer questions reveal information gaps in a listing
- As part of the [Printify Publication Change Approval Process](https://github.com/ajeetswork/ajeets-robots-operations/blob/main/printify-publication-change-approval.md)

This framework is **advisory only** — it does not replace the Publication Change Approval Process, and a passing score here does not authorize a `visible` flag change on its own.

## 2. The six information categories

Rate each category independently. For each field, answer: **Is this information present, accurate, and easy to find in the product listing?**

### 2.1 Physical requirements

Can a customer understand what it takes to physically use, wear, carry, or operate this product?

Check for, where applicable:

- **Weight** — product weight in grams/oz, for items that are carried, worn, or held
- **Dimensions** — length × width × height, or diameter, in both metric and imperial where practical
- **Capacity / volume** — for bags, drinkware, containers (e.g., "13 L", "12 oz / 0.35 L")
- **Grip / handling** — how is the product held, opened, closed, fastened? (e.g., "C-handle with rounded corners", "zippered compartments", "three-button placket")
- **Force / dexterity required** — are buttons, zippers, clasps, lids easy to identify in the description? (describe the mechanism factually; do not rate ease-of-use)
- **Stability / surface requirements** — does the product need a flat surface, two hands, etc.? (state factually if applicable)

> ✅ Good: "Weight: 16g. Size: 1.4" × 2.8". Ergonomic grip."
> ❌ Missing: No weight, no dimensions, no description of how it attaches/holds
>
> ⚠️ Do NOT write: "easy-grip design perfect for users with limited dexterity" — describe the grip factually: "ergonomic grip, 1.4" × 2.8", 16g"

### 2.2 Compatibility limits

Can a customer tell whether this product will work with things they already own, or in the environment they plan to use it in?

Check for, where applicable:

- **Device compatibility** — phone models, case compatibility, OS requirements, port types, etc.
- **Size/fit compatibility** — does apparel fit true to size? What size range is available? Are there sizes missing (e.g., no XS, max 2XL)?
- **Environmental compatibility** — indoor/outdoor use, temperature limits, water resistance, etc.
- **Consumable compatibility** — does it need specific batteries, refills, filters, etc.?
- **Software / platform compatibility** — if applicable
- **"Universal fit" claims** — if the listing says "universal" or "fits all", is that actually true and verifiable from the blueprint catalog? Remove unverifiable universal claims.

> ✅ Good: "Universal fit — works with all smartphones. Compatible with the complete range of smartphone cases."
> ❌ Missing: No mention of what phone sizes/cases it works with
>
> ⚠️ Do NOT write: "accessible to all users regardless of ability" — state compatibility limits factually: "Works with all smartphones. Compatible with all smartphone cases."

### 2.3 Care instructions

Can a customer understand how to clean, maintain, store, or wash this product, and what will damage it?

Check for:

- **Cleaning method** — machine wash / hand wash / wipe clean / dishwasher safe / etc.
- **Temperature limits** — wash temperature, dry temperature, heat sensitivity
- **Chemicals to avoid** — bleach (chlorine vs non-chlorine), fabric softener, harsh detergents, etc.
- **Drying method** — tumble dry low / air dry / lay flat / etc.
- **Ironing / pressing** — allowed or not, temperature if allowed
- **Dry cleaning** — allowed or not
- **Microwave / food safety** — for drinkware / kitchen items
- **Storage notes** — if relevant (e.g., "store flat", "keep dry")

> ✅ Good: "Machine wash cold (max 30°C / 90°F), with similar colors. Non-chlorine bleach as needed. Tumble dry low heat. Do not iron. Do not dryclean."
> ❌ Missing: No care instructions at all
>
> Every physical product listing should include care instructions appropriate to its category. If the Printify blueprint catalog includes care info, copy it into the product description — do not leave it blank.

### 2.4 Sizing

Can a customer determine whether this product will fit them, their space, or their intended use?

Check for, where applicable:

- **Apparel:** Size range offered (e.g., "Youth S–XL"), size chart available, fit type (slim / relaxed / oversized), model height/size reference if available
- **Bags:** Capacity in liters, internal compartment dimensions, laptop/tablet sleeve max device size
- **Drinkware:** Capacity in oz and mL, height/diameter, does it fit standard cup holders?
- **Home goods:** Dimensions (L × W × H), weight
- **Tech accessories:** Physical dimensions, weight, what devices it fits
- **Wearables:** Wrist/neck/head circumference ranges, adjustable or fixed?
- **Missing sizes:** Explicitly note if common sizes are NOT available (e.g., "No XS, max 2XL" for apparel) — this is information customers need, not a defect to hide

> ✅ Good: "Youth sizes S, M, L, XL. 50/50 cotton-poly blend."
> ❌ Missing: No size chart, no dimensions, no capacity stated
>
> If the Printify blueprint includes a size chart, link to it or summarize the key measurements in the product description.

### 2.5 Readability of the listing itself

Can a customer *read and understand* the product listing without confusion?

Check for:

- **Title clarity** — is the product type immediately clear from the title? Is branding consistent? (e.g., "Ajeets Robots – Classic Mug" is clear; "journal" — lowercase, no brand prefix — is not)
- **Description length** — is there enough text to answer basic questions? (Use "words per $10" as a *screening metric only* — a short well-written description can outperform a long one. See Price-to-Information Audit.)
- **Plain language** — is the description written in clear, straightforward language? Avoid jargon, unexplained acronyms, marketing fluff that obscures facts
- **Structure / scannability** — are key facts (material, size, care, price) easy to find, or buried in a wall of text? Use bullet points / sections for: Product features, Materials, Care instructions, Size/fit, Compatibility
- **Image alt text / mockup clarity** — are product images clear, well-lit, showing the product at a usable scale? (Note: Printify mockup image quality is generally outside our direct control, but flag unreadable mockups)
- **Price clarity** — is the price range clear when multiple variants exist? Are all enabled variants intentionally priced?
- **Inconsistent / confusing branding** — title casing, product naming, brand prefix ("Ajeets Robots – " vs "Ajeet Robot mens tee" vs "journal") — inconsistent naming hurts findability and trust

> ✅ Good: Clear title with product type + brand, 50+ word description with Materials / Care / Size sections, consistent pricing
> ❌ Missing: 2-word description ("Phone Stand"), inconsistent branding (lowercase title, missing brand prefix), no materials/fiber content, no care instructions

### 2.6 Other details affecting accessibility of information

Anything else that would prevent a customer from making an informed purchase decision. Examples:

- **Print location / design placement** — where does the Ajeets Robots design appear on the product? Front/center? Small chest logo? All-over print?
- **Color options and contrast** — what colors are available? Is the design readable against the product color? (describe factually: "white garment with black text" — do NOT make contrast/accessibility claims)
- **Print durability** — will the design fade, crack, peel with normal washing? (use Printify blueprint info if available)
- **Country of origin / assembly** — if stated in blueprint (e.g., "Assembled in the USA from globally sourced parts")
- **Included accessories / what's in the box** — cables, chargers, batteries, mounting hardware — is anything required but NOT included?
- **Assembly required?** — does the customer need to assemble anything?
- **Lead time / made-to-order notice** — Printify is print-on-demand; is this clear to the customer?
- **Return/exchange policy** — link to store policy if applicable
- **Allergen / material sensitivity info** — e.g., "OEKO-TEX STANDARD 100 certified", "nickel-free", "latex-free" — include if available in blueprint catalog, do NOT invent
- **Choking hazard / age warnings** — if applicable (small parts, etc.)

> All information must come from the Printify blueprint catalog, the print provider's spec sheet, or verified product testing. **Do not invent materials, dimensions, features, or certifications.**

## 3. Scoring

For each of the six categories above, assign one of:

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| **✅ Complete** | All applicable fields are present, accurate, and easy to find |
| **⚠️ Partial** | Some applicable fields are present, but key information is missing or hard to find |
| **❌ Missing** | Critical information for this category is absent from the listing |
| **N/A** | This category does not apply to this product type (e.g., "Care instructions" for a phone stand with no fabric components — though even then, cleaning instructions may still apply) |

### Overall rating

| Overall | Criteria |
|---------|----------|
| **Ready** | All applicable categories are ✅ Complete. Listing is clear and informative. |
| **Minor Revision** | 1–2 categories are ⚠️ Partial, none are ❌ Missing. Small gaps, quick to fix. |
| **Major Revision** | Any category is ❌ Missing, OR 3+ categories are ⚠️ Partial. Significant information gaps. |
| **Pause Promotion** | Multiple categories are ❌ Missing, OR the listing is actively misleading / contains unverifiable claims. Do not spend promotional budget on this product until remediated. |

**This rating is about information clarity, NOT product quality, and NOT medical accessibility.** A "Pause Promotion" product may be perfectly well-made — its listing just doesn't give customers enough information to buy confidently.

## 4. Medical / accessibility claims — hard rules

Read this section carefully. Violations expose Ajeet's Robots to legal and reputational risk.

### ❌ NEVER do any of the following in a product listing, review, or marketing material:

- Claim a product is "accessible", "ADA compliant", "WCAG compliant", "Section 508 compliant", or compliant with any disability/accessibility standard — unless you have a formal third-party certification specifically for that product, with documentation on file
- Describe a product as suitable for persons with a specific disability, medical condition, or age-related limitation (e.g., "great for arthritis", "perfect for visually impaired users", "autism-friendly")
- Claim a product is a medical device, therapeutic device, assistive technology, or adaptive equipment
- Use terms like: accessible, inclusive (referring to disability), barrier-free, universally designed, disability-friendly, senior-friendly, easy-grip (as a medical claim), ergonomic (unless the Printify blueprint catalog uses that exact word), therapeutic, rehabilitative
- Connect a physical product attribute to a health/medical/disability outcome (e.g., "the large handle helps users with limited dexterity" / "lightweight design reduces strain for elderly users")
- Imply that a product was designed with accessibility or disability inclusion in mind — unless you have documentation that this was an explicit design requirement from the print provider / blueprint manufacturer

### ✅ ALWAYS do this instead:

- **Describe physical attributes factually and measurably:**
  - "Weight: 16g" — not "ultra-lightweight, easy to hold"
  - "C-handle with rounded corners" — not "arthritis-friendly ergonomic handle"
  - "Tear-away label" — not "sensory-friendly, autism-safe tagless design"
  - "1.4" × 2.8"" — not "compact, easy-to-grip size"
  - "Three-button placket" — not "easy fastening for limited dexterity"
  - "Machine wash cold, tumble dry low" — not "easy care, perfect for caregivers"
- **State compatibility limits plainly:** "Works with all smartphones. Compatible with all smartphone cases. Portrait and landscape orientation."
- **List materials exactly as the Printify blueprint states:** "100% polycarbonate" / "50/50 cotton-poly blend" / "100% white ceramic" — do not add, infer, or embellish
- **Include care instructions verbatim from the blueprint catalog** when available
- **Include dimensions, weight, capacity, and sizing in both metric and imperial** where practical
- **If you don't know something, say so or omit it** — do not guess, do not invent. "Material: not specified in blueprint catalog" is better than making up "hypoallergenic bamboo fiber"

### Why this matters

Ajeet's Robots sells print-on-demand apparel, drinkware, bags, and tech accessories through Printify. These are **general consumer products, not medical devices, not assistive technology**. Claiming otherwise — even with good intentions — can:

- Violate FTC truth-in-advertising rules
- Violate FDA medical device regulations
- Create liability if a customer relies on an accessibility claim that turns out to be false
- Mislead customers with disabilities, which is the opposite of being considerate
- Get product listings removed from marketplaces
- Damage trust in the Ajeet's Robots brand

**Being considerate means giving customers clear, accurate, complete information so they can decide for themselves whether a product meets their needs.** That's what this framework is for.
